# S6d — spherical varieties orbit by orbit

Status (2026-09-25): implemented for wonderful varieties whose satellites
are given (`bbcells/frontends/spherical.py`), with complete quadrics and
complete skew forms in every dimension as the first families. Deriving
the satellites from Luna data alone, and general toroidal (non-wonderful)
varieties, remain open (§5).

Conventions are those of `PLAN.md` §1: characters are in simple-root
coordinates of $G$ (the torus of $G_{\mathrm{ad}}$). The base point of $G/P$
has tangent weights $\Phi^-\smallsetminus\Phi_L^-$, as in `flag.py`.

## 1. The obstruction

Up to S6c every front end found all of $X^T$ in the closed orbits, or got it
from a construction (products, blow-ups, restriction). In general $T$-fixed
points also lie in non-closed $G$-orbits. The smallest example is
$\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$ with diagonal $SL_2$: the open orbit
$SL_2/T$ contains $(0,\infty)$ and $(\infty,0)$. The local structure theorem
$X_0 \cong P_u \times Z$, used by the horospherical front end, only sees the
fixed points in $X_0$. Its $W$-translates add nothing: $X_0^T$ lies in the
closed orbits whenever the cone of $Z$ is not a single point.

## 2. Reduction to one base point per orbit

**Lemma.** Let $O = G/H$ be a $G$-orbit.

1. $O^T \neq \emptyset$ if and only if $H$ contains a maximal torus.
2. If $T \subset H$, then $O^T = N_G(T)H/H \cong W/W_H$, where
   $W_H = N_H(T)/T$.
3. The tangent weights at $wH$ are $w(\Phi\smallsetminus\Phi_H)$.

*Proof.* Take $gH\in O^T$. Then $g^{-1}Tg$ and $T$ are maximal tori of $H$,
so $g^{-1}Tg = hTh^{-1}$ for some $h\in H$. Hence $gh \in N_G(T)$, which gives
(1) and (2). The weights of $\mathfrak g/\mathfrak h$ are
$\Phi\smallsetminus\Phi_H$; there is no zero weight because
$\mathfrak t\subset\mathfrak h$. $\square$

On $X$, the normal space $N_x$ to the orbit at the base point is an
$H$-module. Its multiset of $T$-weights is therefore $W_H$-invariant, and

$$X^T = \bigsqcup_{O\,:\,O^T\neq\emptyset} W/W_{H_O},\qquad
T_{w.x_O}X = w(\Phi\smallsetminus\Phi_{H_O}) + w(N_{x_O}).$$

So the complete fixed-point data is determined by one pair
$(\Phi_{H_O}, W_{H_O}; \mathrm{wt}\,N_{x_O})$ per orbit that contains fixed
points. These are the `OrbitDatum` objects, and `assemble` builds the
`FixedPointData` from them.

The coset enumeration uses the fact that $W_H$ is always generated by
reflections: those of the roots of $H$, together with the component
reflections. By Dyer (1990), every coset $wW_H$ has a unique element of
minimal length, and it is characterized by $w(\beta)>0$ for all positive
roots $\beta$ of $W_H$. Compared with taking a minimum over $W_H$, this
took the S6d tests from about 65 s to under 3 s. The switch was checked on every example of
§4, which all still agree exactly.

## 3. Wonderful varieties

Let $X$ be wonderful with spherical roots $\Sigma$, and let $S^p$ be the
simple roots of $P(X)$. The $G$-orbits are the $O_I$ for $I\subseteq\Sigma$,
where $O_I$ is the open orbit of the wonderful subvariety with spherical
roots $I$. Put $S_I = S^p\cup\operatorname{supp} I$. Then $O_I$ is
parabolically induced:

$$O_I = G\times^{P_{S_I}} (L_{S_I}/H_{L,I}),$$

where the *satellite* $L_{S_I}/H_{L,I}$ is cuspidal with spherical roots
$I$. By the lemma, $O_I$ has fixed points if and only if $H_{L,I}$ has full
rank in $L_{S_I}$. In that case, at the base point in the fibre over
$eP_{S_I}$:

- **roots of the stabilizer:**
  $\Phi_H = (\Phi^+\smallsetminus\Phi_{L_{S_I}}) \cup \Phi_{H_{L,I}}$, and
  $W_H = W_{H_{L,I}}$;
- **normal weights:** one weight $\chi_\gamma$ for each $\gamma\in\Sigma\smallsetminus I$,
  namely the normal line of the boundary divisor $D_\gamma$.

**Proposition (normal weights).** Suppose the reflections generating
$W_{H_{L,I}}$ span $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\Phi_{L_{S_I}}$ (condition
(R)). Then $\chi_\gamma$ is the $W_{L_{S_I}}$-average of $-\gamma$, that is,
its orthogonal projection onto $\Phi_{L_{S_I}}^{\perp}$.

*Sketch.* (a) Because $N_H(T)$ acts on the line, $\chi_\gamma$ is
$W_H$-invariant. Under (R) its component in
$\operatorname{span}\Phi_{L_{S_I}}$ is therefore zero. (b) The connected
centre $Z = Z(L_{S_I})^\circ$ is central in $L_{S_I}$, so it acts on the
normal line bundle of $D_\gamma$ along the connected homogeneous fibre
$L_{S_I}/H_{L,I}$ by a constant character. The fibre meets the local
structure slice $S\cong\mathbb{A}^r$ at the point with coordinates
$z_\delta = 1$ for $\delta\in I$ and $z_\delta = 0$ otherwise. Every
$\delta\in I$ is supported in $S_I$, so it is trivial on $Z$, and $Z$ fixes
this point. There $Z$ acts on the coordinate $z_\gamma$ by $-\gamma$. Hence
$\chi_\gamma|_Z = -\gamma|_Z$, and (a) and (b) determine $\chi_\gamma$. The
argument is written in the sign convention of the tests; the orientation of
$P$ versus $P^-$ in the local structure theorem is not spelled out here.
$\square$

When (R) fails, the data $(\Sigma, S^p, H_{L,I})$ does not determine the
normal character. `wonderful_orbits` then refuses rather than guessing. The
minimal case is a satellite with $H_L = T$ in $GL_2$.

*Implemented families.*

| family | $\Sigma$ | $S^p$ | satellites with fixed points |
|---|---|---|---|
| complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ ($PGL_n/PO_n$) | $2\alpha_i$ | $\emptyset$ | $I$ without two adjacent roots; $H_L = N_{L}(T)$ in each $GL_2$ block |
| complete skew forms on $k^{2n}$ ($PGL_{2n}/PSp_{2n}$) | $\alpha_{2i-1}+2\alpha_{2i}+\alpha_{2i+1}$ | $\alpha_1,\alpha_3,\dots$ | only $I=\emptyset$ |
| rank one, full-rank $H$ (tests) | from the S6b.1 normals | from $D = G/Q$ | the open orbit |

For complete quadrics, the orbit of type $I$ has $L_{S_I} = S(\prod GL_{k_j})$
over the blocks of $I$ and $H_{L,I} = S(\prod O_{k_j})$. This has full rank
if and only if every $k_j\le 2$. So the number of fixed points is
$\sum n!/2^{\#\{j: k_j=2\}}$ over the compositions of $n$ into parts 1 and 2:
3, 12, 66, 450, 3690 for $n = 2,\dots,6$.

## 4. Verification

- **Exact fixed-point data**, as multisets of tangent-weight multisets, is
  compared against independent front ends:
  - complete conics (S6c.1, a blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^5$);
  - complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^3$ (S6c.2, Vainsencher's two blow-ups);
  - $\mathbb{P}^1\times\mathbb{P}^1$;
  - $\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^2k^4)$;
  - the S6b.1 completions of $PGL_n/GL_{n-1}$ ($n\le5$), $\mathbb{HP}^n$
    ($n\le 3$), $\mathbb{OP}^2$, $G_2/\langle s\rangle SL_3$, $AQ_{2k}$ and
    $\mathbb{P}^{2k}\smallsetminus Q_{2k-1}$ ($k\le4$).

  Complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^3$ exercise the proposition on orbits
  with two blocks, where both neighbouring roots are subtracted.
- **Point counts** are compared with the orbit decomposition (in
  `oracles.py`, with no BB involved):
  $|X|(q) = \sum_{\text{compositions}} |G/P|(q)\prod_j F(k_j)$.
  Here $F(k)$ counts the smooth quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^{k-1}$ (MacWilliams'
  count of nonsingular symmetric matrices, divided by $q-1$), or
  $|GL_{2k}|/((q-1)|Sp_{2k}|)$ for skew forms. Complete quadrics are checked
  for $n\le 6$ and skew forms for $n\le 4$. Examples:
  - complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^4$:
    $1, 4, 10, 21, 36, 53, 65, 70, 65, \dots, 1$ (dimension 14);
  - complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^5$:
    $1,5,15,37,76,137,216,303,383,441,462,441,\dots,1$ (dimension 20);
  - complete skew forms on $k^6$: $1,2,3,5,7,10,11,12,11,\dots,1$;
  - complete skew forms on $k^8$: 2520 fixed points in dimension 27.

  The quadratic Euler characteristic follows as always. For example,
  complete quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^4$ have $\chi^{\mathbb{A}^1} =
  224\langle1\rangle + 226\langle-1\rangle$, so $\chi(X(\mathbb{R})) = -2$.

## 5. What is not done

1. **Satellites from Luna data.** The satellites $H_{L,I}\supset T$ are
   supplied per family. Deriving them from $(\Sigma, S^p, \mathbf{A})$ alone
   requires two things:
   - deciding when a cuspidal spherical system comes from a full-rank
     subgroup;
   - producing its root data $(\Phi_H, W_H)$.

   For symmetric varieties this should follow from Satake diagrams
   ($L_{S_I}^\theta$). In general it is the construction step of the
   Bravi–Pezzini classification, which is not implemented.
2. **Toroidal, non-wonderful $X$.** Orbits $O_\sigma$ correspond to cones of a
   fan in the valuation cone. They fibre over the orbits of the wonderful
   model by torus fibres. Which cones carry fixed points, and their normal
   weights, would follow from the same lemma, but no front end exists.
   Colored (non-toroidal) varieties would additionally need Gagliardi's
   smoothness criterion (SPEC M6d).
3. **GKM edges** between orbits are not produced. So equivariant cohomology
   (S3.3) and the real incidences (M5) are not available for these
   varieties yet; only the cell counts and the invariants that follow from
   them are.
